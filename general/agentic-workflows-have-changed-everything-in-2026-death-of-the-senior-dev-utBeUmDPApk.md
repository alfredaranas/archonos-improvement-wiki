## TubeOnAI Summary
> - 🔥 “Deep Blue” moment: AI and agentic workflows now outperform many developers at coding, forcing a role shift rather than simple tool adoption.
  - – Reliability has improved from earlier brittle outputs to consistently useful code generation.

- ⚖️ Ethical concerns persist, but AI tooling is becoming standard in software work.
  - – Issues include copyright training, low-paid data labor, and high energy/water use; many adopt while seeking mitigations.

- 🏛️ Architecture risk: AI can create a “Winchester Mystery House” codebase—locally polished, globally incoherent—without principled constraints.
  - – Beyond Cathedral and Bazaar, this third pattern demands stronger human oversight.

- 🧭 Transferable senior skills: managing complexity and optimizing for learning remain essential.
  - – Emphasize sound abstractions, modularity, readability, and tight feedback loops with users.

- 🧪 Prioritize harness engineering over prompt engineering to steer agents.
  - – Build project-specific guides and sensors: tests, CI, linters, policies, telemetry, and executable specs to constrain behavior.

- 🧠 Use BDD and continuous feedback to align AI outputs with user needs.
  - – Instrument for observability and rapid iteration to refine solutions as understanding evolves.

- 👨‍🏫 With a strong harness, newcomers can ship value rapidly even with weak code-reading skills.
  - – Preconditions: capable agents, robust automated checks, and pairing/mentoring from experienced engineers.

- 📈 Trend: LLMs are completing larger tasks unattended, improving on a log-scale (exponential) trajectory.
  - – Implication: hand-writing and reading code may diminish in importance, though risks of architectural decay remain.

- 🛡️ Senior roles shift toward architecture stewardship, harness design, quality gates, and team learning enablement.
  - – Humans set constraints, objectives, and system integrity; agents handle most coding.
