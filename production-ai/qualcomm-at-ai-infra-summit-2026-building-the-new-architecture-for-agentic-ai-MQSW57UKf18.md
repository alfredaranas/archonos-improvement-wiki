## TubeOnAI Summary
> - AI infrastructure must optimize tokens per watt, not just compute capacity, because agentic requests can generate more than a million tokens across 50 to 100 inference calls, compared with about 500 tokens for a chatbot call. - Power demand is rising exponentially, and the speaker warns that worldwide AI power consumption could approach the scale of US energy demand by the end of the decade. He argues the industry cannot rely on new energy sources alone to meet that growth. - The main hardware bottleneck is the memory wall: model parameters are growing about 240 times every two years, while memory capacity roughly doubles. GPUs can sit idle and waste power when memory bandwidth cannot deliver data fast enough. - Qualcomm’s proposed high-bandwidth compute, or HBC, places a DRAM stack directly on top of the compute die to reduce the distance and power cost of moving data. The speaker claims HBC provides over 200 times more memory capacity per watt than SRAM solutions and over six times the bandwidth per watt of high-bandwidth memory. - Qualcomm plans to release its first HBC generation in 2027, claiming an 18-fold bandwidth improvement over legacy solutions, with a second generation targeting 54-fold improvement.
