## Key Takeaways
- Compute costs for AI are rising and can exceed employee costs; rapid data center expansion is reshaping infrastructure demand.
- Modern LLMs improved at tool use and long-horizon tasks, enabling “agentic” patterns where models autonomously plan, call tools, and iterate.
- Advances like self-dialogue, argumentation, and chain-of-thought diversification (e.g., Google-style multi-path reasoning) boost reliability by letting models critique themselves.
- LLMs lack persistent internal memory; they rely on external scratchpads/files to “remember” progress, akin to Memento’s tattoos.
- Agents accumulate skills/instructions and memories over time, externalizing experience much like cultural evolution in humans.
## Apply to ArchonOS
- Add explicit routing criteria for when a request stays single-agent versus becoming an orchestrated multi-agent workflow.
- Require shared state, typed handoffs, and bounded retry budgets so parallel specialists do not drift or duplicate work.
- Measure orchestration value against latency, token cost, and failure rate rather than assuming more agents are better.
